# The interview

One question per message. Offer options when you can, and always allow "something else". Skip what the files already answered and say so in a line instead. The examples are for an invented yoga studio.

## 1. Product

**Ask:** what it is, who uses it and where, and the one job of its most-used screen.
**Options example:** "Where will people mostly use it? · On their phones · At a desk · On a shared tablet at the front desk · A mix (tell me which screen is where)"
**Why we ask:** the device and the main job decide density, type size and layout before any taste does.

## 2. Audience and voice

**Ask:** how technical the people are, how formal the words should be, and the language and local formats (dates, money, times).
**Options example:** "How should the app talk? · Warm and casual · Friendly but brief · Formal and precise"
**Why we ask:** voice shows up in every label; locale formats break layouts when they're found late.

## 3. Mood

**Ask:** 2–3 words for how it should feel, and **what it must never look like**.
**Options example:** "Pick up to three: · Calm · Warm · Crisp · Playful · Premium · Earthy · Clinical" then "What should it never look like? e.g. a generic SaaS dashboard, a gym app, anything neon"
**Why we ask:** mood words choose between equally valid options later. "Never like" rules out whole directions at once.

## 4. Brand

**Ask:** logo, colours, fonts, an existing style guide, design file or tokens. Read whatever exists first, and only ask about the gaps.
**Options example:** "Do you have brand colours? · Yes (paste them or point me to the file) · Only a logo · Nothing yet, propose some (I'll mark them as assumed)"
**Why we ask:** existing brand assets are decisions already made. Inventing over them wastes the person's earlier work.

## 5. References

**Ask:** 2–5 products, sites or images they like. For **each**: what to take, and what to avoid. Look at each one you can open and describe what you see before asking.
**Options example:** "From [reference], what should we take? · The spacing · The type · How the booking works · The colours · Something else" then "And what should we avoid from it?"
**Why we ask:** references turn taste into rules. "Take the generous spacing, avoid the grey-on-grey text" is something a design can follow.
**If they have none:** offer three that fit the mood words and ask the same two questions.

## 6. Constraints

**Ask:** platforms, accessibility needs, light and/or dark, the icon set, and how much motion.
**Options example:** "Anything we must design for? · Older users / large text · Low-end phones · Bright light at a counter · Screen readers · None in particular"
**Why we ask:** constraints found after the first screens mean redrawing them.

## When to stop

Stop when you could design the first screen without guessing on product, audience, mood or brand. Everything still unknown goes to Open questions in `DESIGN.md`.
